About / a Carter Warrens research initiative

ABOUT

Blaque Baux is where Carter Warrens does its systematic research in the open, and puts most of it on the record as rejected, with the reason. What survives is governed, validated, and only then called real.

01

One engine, many directions, and a public trail of what failed.

Blaque Baux is an open, Julia-based systematic-research initiative: a single governed engine steered into a family of strategy sleeves, each pointed at a different corner of the market.

Every idea is tested against the same honest bar. Most do not clear it. Instead of hiding them, we keep them public as a research graveyard: the failed thesis, the constraint, or the risk finding stays visible so it can inform the next test. The handful that survive are built into governed drivers and validated before they are ever described as live. The corpus is the whole record, kept open on GitHub: live systems, work in validation, active research, and null results side by side.

02

The standard: publishing the failures is the point.

Anyone can point an AI at a market now. The edge is understanding what the data actually says, and being willing to show your losses. That discipline runs through everything here:

  • The red scorecard is the pointA rejected strategy, documented with its reason, is a result, not an embarrassment. Most of the corpus is exactly this.
  • Validate before liveNothing is called real on a backtest. It earns a governed paper track, then real money, or it stays research.
  • The tail is the whole storyReturns are fat-tailed, so a bare Sharpe misleads. We test normality first, and weight the crash as heavily as the average.
  • Flag the gap, don't fake itWhere the data isn't there, we say so. No proxy dressed up as the real thing.
03

Governance is the product.

The platform is built in Julia around a venue-agnostic execution controller and a Layer-3 live-money safety gate: preflight checks, idempotency, reconciliation, a high-water-mark and a kill switch. The research may use modern tooling, but no language model sits in the order path: every allocator emits a target book, and reproducible code routes it through the gate.

Performance is judged with a fat-tail toolkit, not a headline number: Jarque–Bera for normality before any mean-variance claim, Jensen's alpha and M² instead of Sharpe alone, and a crisis-correlation check, including how a stream behaves on the market's worst days and the 2008 tell, before anything is trusted to diversify. The point is not a clever signal; it is a book you can actually run.

The master lesson / AI and markets

What 61 Failed Strategies Taught Me About AI and Markets.

After 61 strategies and 3+ years of systematic testing, here is what I know about where AI adds value in markets, and where it does not.

The title is deliberately blunt. Not every sleeve failed, but most original theses changed materially once they met real data, realistic costs, unstable correlations, and the market's tail. Several ideas survived only after the exciting story was removed. What remained was often smaller and less glamorous: a risk overlay, a sizing rule, a diversified ingredient, or a warning not to trade.

The most counter-intuitive lesson was that better prediction was rarely the durable advantage. Geography repeatedly collapsed into US beta plus currency risk. Sophisticated wrappers often became ordinary exposure with an additional fee. Low correlation disappeared during stress. A higher backtested Sharpe sometimes came from concentration in the period's winner, not from a more resilient portfolio. The useful finding was frequently a constraint.

This is where AI helps. It makes the cost of asking another question dramatically lower. It can inspect more hypotheses, translate an idea into reproducible code, find inconsistencies across documents, challenge parameter choices, and accelerate the comparison between a claim and its evidence. Used properly, AI expands the surface area of falsification. It helps a disciplined researcher discover more reasons to say no before capital has to do it.

AI does not decide which problem is worth solving. It does not know whether an apparent relationship is already priced, whether the available proxy represents the real economic exposure, or whether a result can survive liquidity, taxes, crowding, and a regime not present in the sample. Most importantly, it cannot own the consequences of a decision. Those are framing, judgment, and governance problems.

What surprised me most was how often the research answered the wrong question correctly. An optimizer could identify the best historical weights without showing that those weights were stable. A model could classify stress after it arrived without providing a tradable lead. A simulation could improve average return while relocating risk into one unrepeated event. In each case, computational success concealed decision failure. The hard work was not producing the answer. It was defining what an answer had to earn before anyone could act on it. That bar cannot be delegated to the model.

The corpus changed my view of intelligence in markets. Intelligence is not the volume of ideas a system can produce. It is the quality of the questions surrounding a decision: What would disprove this? Which risk is being relocated rather than removed? What happens in the worst five days? Who can stop the process? What evidence earns promotion from research to validation, and from validation to live?

The master lesson: AI is most valuable as an instrument for structured doubt. It can make research faster, broader, and more internally consistent. It becomes dangerous when speed is mistaken for understanding, confidence for causality, or generated output for accountable judgment.

That is why the graveyard stays public. The rejected sleeves show the framing process, not just the winners left after selection. They make the surviving system more credible because the record exposes what was tested, what broke, and why. Markets do not reward the appearance of intelligence for long. They reward decisions that remain coherent after the story fails.

Failure Postmortems / framing before tooling

When the implementation worked and the decision system did not.

Anonymized composite case studies of recurring AI implementation failures. Identifying details are removed. The purpose is to expose the hard framing question that should have been asked before scale.

Postmortem / 01

The metric without a decision.

What happened
An attrition model ranked employees accurately enough to impress the project team, but no one had defined an authorized intervention. Managers began treating a probability as a verdict.
The question not asked
What decision changes at each threshold, who owns it, and what protects the person affected by a false positive?
Takeaway
A prediction without a governed action is not a product. It is an unmanaged liability.
Postmortem / 02

The copilot without a boundary.

What happened
A generative assistant increased drafting speed, then overwhelmed review queues. Unverified output crossed a control boundary because productivity had been measured before accountability.
The question not asked
What may the model draft, what requires named human approval, and what may never enter production?
Takeaway
Faster generation magnifies weak controls. Throughput is not the same thing as completed, trusted work.
Postmortem / 03

The forecast in the order path.

What happened
A model cleared its historical benchmark and entered an allocation process. A regime change weakened its features while its confidence remained high, and no independent shutdown path existed.
The question not asked
What evidence can stop the model, and can the system fail safely when its assumptions no longer hold?
Takeaway
Safe AI needs both a falsification path and a shutdown path. Accuracy alone supplies neither.

These are composite patterns intended to preserve confidentiality while making the operating failure visible. They do not identify or describe any single client engagement.

Using the work / frequently asked questions

How to read, reproduce, and apply the research.

01Who is BLAQUE BAUX intended for?+
BLAQUE BAUX is built for researchers, quantitative developers, portfolio and risk professionals, students, family offices, asset managers, private-equity teams, hedge funds, and serious independent investors who want to study how systematic strategies are constructed, tested, rejected, governed, and monitored.
02How should I use the research and strategy catalog?+
Use the site as a research map rather than a menu of trade recommendations. Begin with the thesis and assumptions, inspect the implementation and evidence, review the risk and failure conditions, and reproduce the result independently before adapting anything to your own constraints.
03What is the difference between Labs, Corpus, Block, Capstone, and Tracker?+
Labs presents interactive studies. Corpus is the complete research record, including live, validation, active research, and null results. Block is the derivatives strategy reference. Capstone studies portfolio construction and variations above the individual sleeves. Tracker shows simulated sleeve performance against relevant benchmarks.
04Which tools and languages does the platform use?+
Julia is the primary research and governed-execution language. Python is used where its data, automation, or integration ecosystem is useful. The governing principle is reproducibility and clear interfaces, not loyalty to a language.
05How can Julia and Python fit into an existing workflow?+
Teams can use Python for data preparation, notebooks, orchestration, and established analytics while using Julia for research components, simulation, optimization, and controlled execution. Exchange data through explicit files, tables, APIs, or other versioned interfaces so each stage can be tested and reproduced independently.
06Can the strategies be used directly in a live portfolio?+
No strategy on the site should be copied directly into a live portfolio. The material is educational research, not individualized investment advice. Users must independently validate data, assumptions, costs, liquidity, tax treatment, legal constraints, implementation details, and suitability for their own objectives and risk limits.
07What do Live, Validation, Research, and Null mean?+
Live identifies work that has passed the project’s governance requirements and is being tracked under its stated operating conditions. Validation identifies candidates under further observation. Research identifies active investigations. Null is the public graveyard for ideas that failed, lacked sufficient evidence, or were rejected for risk or implementation reasons.
08How can researchers reproduce or extend the work?+
Start with the linked repository and its documentation. Preserve the original assumptions and baseline before changing parameters, data, or portfolio rules. Record each change, test it out of sample where possible, and evaluate tail behavior, implementation costs, and failure conditions rather than relying on a single headline metric.
09Is BLAQUE BAUX investment advice?+
No. BLAQUE BAUX provides educational research, software, documentation, and illustrative analysis. It does not provide personalized investment, legal, tax, or accounting advice, and it does not recommend that any person buy, sell, or hold a security or strategy.
10Will BLAQUE BAUX become an investment manager or fund?+
BLAQUE BAUX is currently a research and technology initiative, not an investment adviser, broker-dealer, fund, or asset manager. No future structure is being promised. Any regulated investment activity would be established separately and communicated only after the appropriate legal, operational, and regulatory framework was in place.
11Who owns and operates BLAQUE BAUX?+
BLAQUE BAUX is a Carter Warrens research initiative founded by Kareem Williams. Carter Warrens provides the program-management, governance, transformation, and execution discipline behind the work.
12How can an institution work with Carter Warrens?+
Carter Warrens works with organizations that need help structuring research, governance, delivery, risk controls, or technical operating models. Contact ceo@carterwarrens.com to discuss an advisory or collaboration need.
